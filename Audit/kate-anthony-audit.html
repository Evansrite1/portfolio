Part one

What the business is doing today

Six findings, in the order a new visitor from Instagram runs into them.

01

Your bio is still selling a retreat that's over

The third line of your bio says "Sedona Retreat Sept 10–13 | Save your spot 👇". Those dates passed more than two weeks ago, and your own retreat page says September is sold out. A new visitor sees an old event, while the most persuasive fact you have, that it sold out, isn't mentioned anywhere on Instagram.

Bio today: 🌵 Sedona Retreat Sept 10–13 | Save your spot👇
Retreat page today: September 2026 is sold out. Now accepting reservations for April 2027
02

There's no way to tap through to the retreat

"kateanthony.com/unbreakable" sits in your bio as plain text. Instagram doesn't turn bio text into a link, so it can't be tapped. The only tappable link is your Instagram Links page, and its seven buttons cover the podcast, Phoenix Rising, the Toolkit, The D Word, private coaching and two "start here" pages. The Unbreakable retreat isn't one of them. So "Save your spot 👇" points at a page with no retreat on it.

Also on that page: "Join Phoenix Rising" and "Getting divorced? Start here" both open the same Phoenix Rising page.
03

Instagram reaches a small slice of your audience

Your podcast is the engine of this business. Instagram isn't pulling its weight yet. Across your five most recent posts, likes ranged from 4 to 34 on 26.7K followers. A commonly cited healthy range is 1 to 3% of followers engaging per post.

Comments are also limited on your recent posts. Given the topic, that protection makes complete sense. It does cost reach, though, because comments are one of the main signals Instagram uses to decide how widely to show a post.

04

No free way onto your email list

4.5 million downloads means a huge number of women who already trust your voice. But on your homepage, your podcast "Start here" page and your Should I Stay or Should I Go? page, I couldn't find a free guide, quiz or checklist to join your list. The first thing anyone can get from you is the $50 book. Listeners who aren't ready to buy simply leave, and you can't reach them again.

05

The podcast is hard to find by search

You win "high-conflict divorce coach for women". For the broader search "divorce podcast", the first ten results were The Divorce Podcast with Kate Daly, the Goodpods and FeedSpot "best divorce podcasts" lists, and other shows. The Divorce Survival Guide wasn't on the page, even with 300+ episodes behind it.

06

Small technical items on the site

The homepage's main heading tag is empty, so search engines don't get your headline "High-Conflict Divorce Changes the Rules" as the page's main heading. Sections such as the retreat price and "How it works" fade in only as you scroll, so quick skimmers and some tools see blank space first. And the homepage took about nine seconds to fully load in my test. None of these is urgent. Together they're worth a tidy-up.

Part two

What it should be doing

Grouped by what matters most: more visibility, more influence, more money.

More visibility Be found

  • Update the bio today. Replace the September line with "Sept retreat sold out. April 2027 open 👇". I've written three ready-to-paste versions for you.
  • Make the retreat the first button on your Instagram Links page: "Unbreakable Retreat · April 29 to May 2, 2027 · September sold out".
  • Turn every podcast episode into Instagram content: one clip reel and one quote carousel per episode, each ending with "full episode in bio". You have 300+ episodes of material.
  • Get on the lists that rank. Ask to be added to the Goodpods and FeedSpot divorce podcast rankings, and trade guest spots with the shows that appear for "divorce podcast".
  • Open comments on educational posts and use Instagram's keyword filters to hide anything hurtful. Keep personal posts limited if you prefer.

More influence Be trusted

  • Say the proof out loud: "4.5M downloads", "New York Times recommended", "retreat sold out" in your bio, pinned posts and at the top of each program page.
  • Share retreat stories. A short post from a September attendee (with permission) will sell April better than any description.
  • Do more collaborations. Your pinned collab with The Happily Never After is a good example. One collab reel a month puts you in front of an audience that already cares about the topic.
