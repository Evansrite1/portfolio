How to record multi-track audio in your browser

  1. Put on headphones. If you record over other tracks with speakers, the microphone hears the playback.
  2. Add a backing track (optional). Drop a WAV or MP3 of the beat, guitar or guide vocal onto the page. It becomes track 1.
  3. Choose your input. Press Allow microphone and find inputs, pick the microphone or audio interface, and choose Mono or Stereo. Press Test input and speak to check the level meter.
  4. Place the playhead and press Record. Recording starts at the playhead, the other tracks play along, and a new track is added when you stop. Use the count-in if you want a few beeps first.
  5. Fix the timing if needed. If the new take sits slightly late against the backing track, raise the latency compensation and record again.
  6. Export. Download a mixdown as WAV or MP3, or every track as its own file in a ZIP.

Echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain control are off by default because they damage music. Turn them on only for voice calls or very noisy rooms.

Open audio from Audacity, Reaper and other editors

This tool cannot open Audacity project files (.aup3) or Reaper project files (.rpp), because those files hold the project layout and not the sound itself. What does work is exporting your tracks as audio files and adding them here.

  • Audacity: choose File, Export, Export Multiple, pick WAV, and split by tracks. You get one WAV per track.
  • Reaper: choose File, Render, set the source to Stems (selected tracks), pick WAV, and render.
  • Any other editor or recorder: export each track as WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC or OGG. Drop all the files in together.

Each file becomes a track that starts at zero. If your stems all start at the same moment, as stems from a render do, they line up automatically. From there you can trim, split, fade, move, set volume and pan, record new parts and export a new mix.

What you can do with it

  • Edit: move clips in time, drag the edges to trim, split at the playhead, add fade in and fade out, reverse, change gain and normalize.
  • Mix: set volume and pan for each track, mute and solo, rename and colour tracks.
  • Record: overdub on top of other tracks with latency compensation, a level meter and an optional count-in. With a multi-channel audio interface that your browser exposes, you can record each input channel to its own track.
  • Export: WAV in 16 or 24 bit, MP3, a single track, or all tracks as a ZIP of WAV files, at 44.1 or 48 kHz.

Everything happens on your own computer. Your audio is not uploaded, so it works for private demos and unreleased songs.

Tips for a clean result

  • Keep recording levels so the meter peaks below the red zone. You can raise the volume later but you cannot repair clipping.
  • Record at the same sample rate you plan to export, usually 48 kHz for video and 44.1 kHz for music.
  • Use short fades of 5 to 20 ms at cut points to avoid clicks.
  • Save your work by exporting. The page does not keep your project when you close it, so export a ZIP of tracks before you leave.
  • Use Chrome or Edge on a computer. Very long projects use a lot of memory, so keep sessions to a reasonable length.

Frequently asked questions

Is this audio editor free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on how many files you edit or export.

Are my recordings and files uploaded?

No. Files are decoded, edited, recorded and exported inside your browser on your own computer. Nothing is sent to a server.

Can I open an Audacity or Reaper project?

No. Project files (.aup3 and .rpp) only describe a project, so this tool cannot read them. Export your tracks from Audacity or Reaper as WAV stems and add those files here instead.

Can I record several tracks at once?

You can record one input at a time while the other tracks play. If your audio interface shows more than two channels to the browser, you can record each channel to its own track. Browsers and operating systems often expose only two channels, so this depends on your device and drivers.

Why is my recording slightly late?

Sound takes a moment to travel through your audio hardware and browser. Use the latency compensation setting to shift new recordings earlier so they line up, and wear headphones.

Which file formats can I add and export?

You can add WAV, MP3, M4A, AAC, FLAC, OGG, Opus and AIFF files, plus the audio from many video files. You can export WAV in 16 or 24 bit, MP3, or a ZIP of one file per track.

Does it save my project?

No. Close the page and the project is gone. Export a ZIP of your tracks to keep your work, then add those files again later.
